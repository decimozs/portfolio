title: "Why Reading My Notes Wasn't Enough"
description: "How Recall turns notes into quizzes and flashcards that make learning foundations part of a daily rhythm."
date: 2026-08-03
---

I kept collecting notes about software engineering and computer science, but collecting them did not mean I could remember them. When I returned to an old topic, I often recognized the words without being able to explain the idea clearly.

This happens easily when learning is squeezed between work, responsibilities, and everything else that fills a normal day. A concept can feel familiar after reading it once, then disappear when I need to explain it in a meeting or use it in a project.

Recall started as a way to make learning more active. It turns my notes into quizzes and flashcards that help me test what I remember instead of simply reading the same material again.

![Recall dashboard](/blog/recall/Screenshot%202026-08-03%20at%2012.58.55%E2%80%AFPM.png)

Living in Manila, my time is often fragmented. A long commute, a delayed meeting, or a quiet few minutes before work can create small spaces for learning, but those spaces are easy to waste when I do not know what to study next.

On a busy day, I can open Recall between tasks, review a few questions, and return to the concepts I have been studying without rebuilding my context from scratch. A short review during a break can keep an important idea fresh before I need to use it in a real project.

The same routine works after work. Instead of opening another collection of tabs and trying to decide where to begin, I can review a small set of questions about a topic I want to strengthen. The session does not need to be long to be useful. It only needs to make me retrieve the idea again.

Taking a quiz makes the gaps visible. It is easy to believe that I understand a concept while reading about it. Answering a question forces me to find out whether I can actually recall it.

![Recall quiz](/blog/recall/Screenshot%202026-08-03%20at%201.00.14%E2%80%AFPM.png)

Flashcards give me another way to review. I can reveal an answer, decide whether I remembered it, and continue reviewing without turning the process into a large study session.

![Recall flashcards](/blog/recall/Screenshot%202026-08-03%20at%201.00.42%E2%80%AFPM.png)

This is where upskilling becomes more practical for me. Learning a new area can feel overwhelming when it is treated as one large goal, such as mastering distributed systems, improving database knowledge, or becoming more comfortable with AI systems. Recall gives me a way to break that goal into smaller concepts that I can revisit over time.

Before a technical interview, I can use it to revisit the foundations I have not practiced recently. Before starting a new project, I can review the ideas that are likely to matter. Even after finishing a project, I can turn the notes and decisions from that experience into material I can return to later.

The agent harness helps with the part I usually postpone. It turns the notes I already have into questions and practice sessions that fit into the day. I still have to think, answer, and work through the concepts myself, but I do not have to manually prepare every review session before I can begin.

Recall is not a replacement for building real projects, reading carefully, or asking other people for help. It also cannot fix unclear notes on its own. The quality of a review depends on the quality of the material I give it and on whether I am honest when I do not remember an answer.

The useful part is the repetition. A concept reviewed once may feel familiar, but a concept revisited across several days becomes easier to retrieve when the situation calls for it. That difference matters when the goal is not just to finish a course, but to carry the knowledge into daily work.

The point is not to turn every spare minute into another productivity task. It is to make learning easier to return to when the day is busy. A few questions during a commute, a short flashcard session after work, or a review before starting a project can gradually build a stronger foundation.

With Recall, learning becomes less about waiting for a perfect schedule and more about making use of the small moments that already exist.
